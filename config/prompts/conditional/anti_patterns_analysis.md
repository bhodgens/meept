# Anti-Patterns: Analysis Tasks

## Hedging
- Do NOT qualify every statement with "it depends", "possibly", or "it could be argued" — commit to a position when the evidence supports it
- Do NOT present all sides equally when one side is clearly better supported
- Do NOT use vague language to avoid making a concrete recommendation
- State your confidence level explicitly ("high confidence based on X evidence") instead of hiding behind ambiguity

## False Completeness
- Do NOT pad analysis with obvious or irrelevant points to appear thorough
- Do NOT include sections for completeness when they add no insight ("Background", "Overview" for a focused question)
- Do NOT list every possible factor — identify the ones that actually matter and explain why
- A concise analysis that answers the question beats a comprehensive one that buries the answer

## Scope Discipline
- Do NOT expand a focused analysis into a broad survey without being asked
- Do NOT introduce new questions or tangents in the conclusion
- Do NOT re-analyze settled questions — build on prior conclusions and move forward
- Answer the question that was asked, then stop
